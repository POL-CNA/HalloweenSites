He walked into the courtyard. The fountain was dry. Yet the sound continued.

Drip.

Drip.

followed it to a small wooden door beneath the staircase. He had noticed the door when he moved in but assumed it led to a storage room. There was no key. The next morning, he asked an elderly neighbor about it. The old man became strangely quiet.

“You shouldn't open that.”

“Why?”

“Because some doors are locked for a reason.”

Sami smiled. That night, he broke the lock. Behind the door was a narrow staircase descending into darkness. At the bottom was a room. There was nothing inside except an old stone well. Sami shone his flashlight into it. He couldn't see the bottom. Then something whispered from below.

“Sami.”

He froze. The voice sounded like his mother. His mother had been dead for six years.

"Sami…”

He stepped backward. The voice came again.

“I'm cold.”

Sami ran upstairs and slammed the door. The next morning, he poured concrete over the doorway. For three days, nothing happened. On the fourth night, he woke to knocking. Not from the basement. From inside his bedroom wall. Knock. Knock. Knock. He pressed his ear against the wall. A woman's voice whispered:

“You closed the door.”

Sami left the house that night. He never returned. Years later, workers renovating the building discovered something beneath the courtyard. There was no basement. No staircase. No room. And no well. Only solid earth. But beneath the earth, they found an old wooden door. On its inside surface were dozens of scratches. And beneath the scratches, carved into the wood, was one sentence:

“DO NOT LET THEM KNOW YOU CAN HEAR THEM”
