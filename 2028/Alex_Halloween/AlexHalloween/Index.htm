The Black Forest Lantern

On Halloween night, in a small village beside Germany's Black Forest, the children gathered around a bonfire. They laughed, told ghost stories, and carried carved pumpkins through the dark streets.

But the oldest woman in the village, Frau Adler, watched them with worried eyes.

"Do not enter the forest after midnight," she warned. "Especially if you see a lantern moving between the trees."

The children laughed. They thought it was just another spooky tale.

Except for Erik.

At fifteen years old, Erik wanted to prove he wasn't afraid. So when the village clock struck twelve, he slipped away from the festival and headed toward the forest.

The moment he crossed beneath the towering pines, the sounds of the village disappeared.

No music. No voices. Only silence.

Then he saw it-A golden lantern glowing deep among the trees. Someone was carrying it.

The figure wandered slowly through the fog.

Erik followed.

Every time he got closer, the lantern seemed to drift farther away. Minutes turned into hours. The forest grew darker. The trees twisted into strange shapes.

Soon Erik realized something was wrong. The figure carrying the lantern had never once turned around.

It never stumbled. Never changed speed. Almost as if it already knew he was following. Then, without warning, it stopped.

The lantern hung motionless in the darkness. Erik took another step.

The figure slowly turned. His stomach dropped.

It wasn't a person.

Beneath the hood was a skull with empty eye sockets burning with pale blue flames. The lantern illuminated dozens of gravestones hidden among the trees. Names covered every stone. Hundreds of names. Some dated back centuries.

One looked newer than the rest.

His blood froze. "No..." he whispered.

The skull tilted its head. Then dozens of lanterns suddenly ignited around him.

One by one.

A circle of ghostly lights. And beside every lantern stood another silent figure.

Villagers.

Children.

Hunters.

Travelers. All long dead.

The skull raised a bony finger and pointed toward the empty grave. The earth beside it began to move.

Something was climbing out.

Something with Erik's face.

Erik ran.

Branches clawed at his clothes. The dead whispered his name from every direction. The lantern light followed him through the forest.

Faster.

Closer.

Closer.

Finally, he burst out of the trees and collapsed in the village square. The Halloween festival had ended. The bonfire was cold. The streets were empty. A church bell rang in the distance. Erik staggered to a nearby house and knocked frantically.

An old man opened the door. "Please," Erik gasped. "Help me."

The man stared in confusion.

"I'm sorry," he said. "Who are you?"

"My name is Erik Schneider."

The old man's face turned pale. "That can't be." He pointed to a newspaper hanging on the wall.

Erik looked.

There was a photograph of himself. The headline read:

LOCAL BOY MISSING IN BLACK FOREST FOR 50 YEARS

The date was fifty years in the future.

As the newspaper slipped from his trembling hands, a familiar silver light appeared behind him.

A lantern.

Moving slowly through the fog. Waiting to take him back into the forest.

Forever.
